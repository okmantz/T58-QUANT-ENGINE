# T58 three objective-specific bots: comparison report (2026-11-20)

**Bottom line.** Only Bot C held up. Bot C compounds net wealth, and on the sealed Mar–Nov 2026 holdout it beat the shipped Net Profit Compounder at the same risk, and it beat buy-and-hold. Bot A is the evaluation passer, and on the holdout it did worse than the shipped Evaluation Passer. Bot B is the payout hunter, and it was no better than the shipped Payout Hunter in research. The holdout has too few trades to judge Bot B. The one finding worth acting on for B is to run it on Lucid Flex, not Pro.

## How it was tested
- **Rules:** Lucid 50K as of 2026-11-20 (research/LUCID_RULES_2026-11-20.md):
  - $2,000 end-of-day trailing loss limit, locking at $50,100 once an end-of-day balance reaches $52,100.
  - $1,200 soft daily limit, $3,000 target and 40 micros.
  - Pro payouts: $500 of cycle profit, best day at most 40% of the cycle, and the balance must stay above $52,100.
  - Flex payouts: five days of at least $150 each, and the payout is at most 50% of profit, capped at $2,000.
  - Counted as a breach: any account whose room above the floor falls under $250, because it can no longer trade a real stop.
- **Splits:**
  - train: 2020–23
  - val: 2024
  - testc: 2025-01 to 2026-02, contaminated because earlier work had read it. It is reported but was never used to choose.
  - holdout: 2026-03 to 11-19, sealed with SHA-256 before discovery and opened once after the picks were hashed (research/FROZEN_PICKS.*).
- **Selection rule:** declared before every experiment as the best min(train, val). One correction was made before the holdout opened: Bot A's ORB legs now pass through the repo arbiter.
- **Execution:**
  - Signal at the bar close, entry at the next open plus 1 tick, and stops filled 1 tick worse.
  - When a stop and a target fall in the same bar, the stop is assumed hit first. Flat by 15:45 CT.
  - Costs of $1 per micro.
  - Shipped legs use the repo engine's own trades. Sizing uses the repo's own `decide_risk`, so the configs that ship are the configs that were tested.
- **Control:** random-direction twins with the same bars, stops, targets and weights (the Transfer Test).

## Bot A: evaluation passer, Lucid 50K Pro (P(pass within 30 days))
| Book | train p30 | val p30 | testc p30 | val breach60 | Holdout Jun–Nov p30 (breach60) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shipped Evaluation Passer | 43.8% | 54.6% | 34.0% | 42% | **28.7%** (12.5%) |
| **BOT_A** (shipped legs + Compression ORB NQ/ES, flatter governor) | 52.7% | 52.3% | 30.5% | 46% | 16.2% (37.5%) |
| Shipped legs with random ORB twins instead | ~41% | ~50% | ~37% | ~47% | — |
| ORB legs alone (best sizing) | 32% | 33% | 18% | ~2% | — |
| Shipped legs on Flex instead of Pro | 9.5% | 9.2% | 2.7% | 42% | — |
- **What happened:** the gain over the shipped bot came from train only. On the holdout, the Nasdaq ORB lost 0.13R a trade over 24 trades, and Bot A passed about half as often.
- **Holdout limits:** only legs with holdout data are in either book. No NQ 1-minute data exists for 2026, so Context Drive Long is missing from both, and Open Drive has only Oct 30 to Nov 19. That leaves about 6 independent 30-day windows.
- **Verdict:** keep the shipped Evaluation Passer. BOT_A ships only as a research config, marked as such.

## Bot B: payout hunter (P(first payout within 90 funded days), breach before a payout)
| Book | Plan | train | val | testc | val breach before payout | Lifecycle net $/yr (train, val) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shipped Payout Hunter | Pro | 42.8% | 61.4% | 22.4% | 0% | $3,293 / $3,423 |
| Shipped Payout Hunter governor | **Flex** | 83.8% | 95.5% | 94.8% | 0% | $1,349 / $1,414 |
| **BOT_B** (shipped legs, Flex, governor retuned) | Flex | 89.5% | 93.2% | 84.5% | 3.4% | $1,924 / $1,752 |
| Shipped legs + random 1R twins (control) | Flex | 64–87% | 73–99% | 36–81% | 0–16% | — |
- **Flex vs Pro:** Flex pays far more often than Pro. Pro pays bigger cheques, so its yearly net is higher.
- **What didn't work:** ORB 1R legs added nothing, and random twins scored the same as the real legs. The retuned governor is better on train, not on val or testc.
- **Holdout:** too sparse to judge. The legs with data made 42 trades in 8 months, so no book reached a payout.
- **Verdict:** run the shipped Payout Hunter on Flex. BOT_B is the same legs on Flex with a governor that did not prove better.

## Bot C: net profit compounder, unrestricted account (all books scaled to 20% annual volatility on train, so leverage doesn't decide)
| Book | train Calmar | val Calmar | testc Calmar | Holdout Mar 2–Nov 19 return, max DD, Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shipped NET mix (6 intraday ×2 + Gate 27 ES/GC) | 5.95 | 42.0 | 8.1 | +4.6%, 5.4%, 0.77 |
| **BOT_C** (Kelly-shaped weights + Compression ORB ES/NQ) | 8.4 | 34.0 | 10.4 | **+9.5%, 5.2%, 1.08** |
| BOT_C with the drawdown throttle | 8.7 | 30.5 | 10.1 | annualised 9.0% (vs 12.9% unthrottled), DD 4.9% |
| ES/NQ/GC SMA150 trend (baseline) | 1.10 | 3.4 | 5.4 | — |
| ES buy-and-hold (1×) | 0.45 | 2.6 | 0.72 | +4.6%, 8.6%, 0.47 |
- **Weights:** fractional-Kelly direction (shrunk inverse covariance of train daily returns), capped by a volatility budget. Raw half Kelly implied drawdowns of 47–71%, so it was rejected.
- **What drove the holdout gain:**
  - The S&P Compression ORB made +0.22R a trade over 42 trades. That beats 91.5% of 200 random-direction twins, but t = 1.3 is not significant.
  - Gold trend lost 7%.
  - The Nasdaq ORB lost.
- **Limits:**
  - The sleeves are summed without the bot arbiter, for both books.
  - Gold data ends Jul 31.
  - The NQ 1-minute legs are missing.
  - Raw research CAGRs are large because they compound many 1%-risk intraday trades. They are not forecasts.
- **Verdict:** BOT_C is the only pick that beat its shipped counterpart out of sample. It is a modest, unproven edge, mostly one leg.

## What is weak
- The holdout is short: about 6 pass windows, about 8 months for C, and too thin for B.
- Missing data: NQ has no data for Mar–May 2026, and the NQ 1-minute legs can't be scored at all.
- testc was contaminated.
- The Compression ORB edge rests mainly on ES. NQ turned negative in 2026.
- Lucid's rules come from its help centre (dated file). Fees come from secondary sources.
